BANKRUPTCY IN MEMPHIS & SHELBY COUNTY

A financial reset starts
with understanding the choices.

Bankruptcy is a legal process, not a promise that every debt disappears or every asset can be kept. The right approach depends on your income, property, debts, goals, and filing history.

Chapter 7 bankruptcy

For eligible debtors, Chapter 7 may discharge qualifying debts. A trustee administers the case, and nonexempt property can be sold. Eligibility, exemptions, and debts that survive filing need careful review.

Chapter 13 bankruptcy

Chapter 13 allows eligible individuals with regular income to propose a court-approved repayment plan, generally lasting three to five years. Plan requirements and what you must pay depend on your circumstances.

Credit cards & medical bills

Review unsecured debts and discuss whether they may qualify for discharge or treatment in a repayment plan.

Collection calls & lawsuits

Discuss the collection activity, any court papers, and whether bankruptcy may address the underlying debt or provide a stay of collection activity.

Garnishment & judgments

Review the judgment, garnishment paperwork, and timing. Some collection activity may be affected by a bankruptcy filing, but exceptions and limits apply.

Mortgage & vehicle concerns

Discuss missed payments, foreclosure notices, repossession concerns, and how secured debts and your goals affect the available options.

Tell Jay about urgent dates before assuming there is time

A sale date, repossession concern, garnishment, or lawsuit can require prompt attention. Bankruptcy’s automatic stay has exceptions and limitations, including rules that may affect repeat filings. Contacting the firm alone does not stop collection activity.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Before you reach out.

Do I need Chapter 7 or Chapter 13?

That depends on your eligibility, income, debts, property, goals, and filing history. An appointment allows Jay to discuss the differences and assess which options warrant closer review.

Will bankruptcy eliminate every debt?

No. Some debts are not dischargeable or require additional legal review. The treatment of a particular debt depends on its nature, the bankruptcy chapter, and the facts of the case.

Will I lose my home or car?

There is no blanket answer. Equity, exemptions, loan terms, payment status, and the proposed bankruptcy chapter all matter. Discuss your specific property before making a filing decision.

Will calling Jay stop collection or foreclosure?

No. Contacting the firm does not itself stop collection activity or create bankruptcy protection. A filing may provide an automatic stay, subject to exceptions and limits. Tell the firm about any urgent date immediately.

What does it cost to file bankruptcy?

Attorney fees, court costs, counseling requirements, and payment terms depend on the proposed case. The firm will explain the scope of services and applicable fees before you decide whether to engage Jay.
