EMPLOYMENT LAW IN MEMPHIS & SHELBY COUNTY

Workplace problems are personal.
The facts deserve attention.

Your job can affect your finances, health, and family. Jay starts by understanding the events, the people involved, and the records available. Whether a legal claim exists depends on the facts and the law that applies.

Workplace discrimination

Concerns about hiring, firing, pay, promotion, or other employment decisions involving race, sex, religion, national origin, disability, age, or another legally protected characteristic.

Sexual harassment & hostile work environments

Unwelcome sexual conduct or harassment connected to a protected characteristic. The nature, severity, frequency, and context of the conduct matter.

Retaliation

Concerns about punishment after reporting discrimination, participating in an investigation, requesting an accommodation, or exercising other protected workplace rights.

Termination & wrongful discharge

Review of the stated reason for a firing, the surrounding events, and any relevant contract or legal protection. An unfair firing is not necessarily an unlawful one.

Unpaid wages & overtime

Missing pay, off-the-clock work, overtime concerns, or questions about a worker’s classification. Coverage, actual job duties, hours worked, and pay records require review.

Disability accommodations

Questions about accommodation requests, employer responses, and workplace barriers under applicable disability laws.

Pregnancy & protected leave

Concerns about pregnancy-related treatment, accommodation, or family and medical leave. Eligibility and employer coverage depend on the law and your circumstances.

Severance & employment agreements

Review of proposed severance, releases, restrictive covenants, or other employment terms. Share the agreement and any deadline before deciding whether to sign.

Understand the reason and the context

Tennessee generally follows at-will employment, but legal protections and contractual rights can limit an employer’s actions. Jay evaluates the particular events rather than assuming every difficult workplace situation creates a claim.

START WITH WHAT YOU KNOW

Facing a workplace issue?
Bring the timeline.

What to have ready

Write down the key dates, employer name, your role, what happened, and who was involved. Include any termination date, internal complaint, accommodation request, or agency filing.

Gather relevant records you lawfully possess, such as pay stubs, time records, policies, evaluations, messages, contracts, and any EEOC or other agency correspondence. Ask the firm how to provide documents securely.

A brief initial inquiry is enough. Avoid sending sensitive medical information, personnel records, or detailed accusations in a text message.

Deadlines need prompt attention

Employment matters can involve short deadlines and agency procedures. An internal complaint or negotiation generally does not extend an EEOC charge deadline. Tell Jay about any agency notice, proposed release, or court date immediately.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Before you reach out.

Does an unfair firing mean I have a legal claim?

Not necessarily. Tennessee generally follows at-will employment. A claim may depend on discrimination, retaliation, a contract, or another legal protection. Jay will review the reason given and the surrounding facts.

Can I contact Jay while I am still employed?

Yes. You can request an appointment about a current workplace concern, a proposed agreement, or questions about your options. Tell the firm about any upcoming meeting or deadline.

Do I need to file with the EEOC first?

Some discrimination claims require an agency charge before a lawsuit; other employment claims follow different processes. The proper procedure and deadline depend on the claim, employer, and applicable law. Share any filings or notices you already have.

What if I have already received a severance agreement?

Provide the complete proposed agreement and any stated deadline. The terms may affect legal rights and future choices. Jay can discuss whether agreement review fits the matter and the proposed engagement.

Can you tell me what my case is worth?

That requires an individual review of the facts, applicable law, evidence, losses, and available remedies. Neither a recovery nor a dollar amount is guaranteed.

How do fees work?

Fees and expenses depend on the type of matter and work proposed. The firm will discuss the scope, fee arrangement, and engagement terms before you decide whether to hire Jay.
