Your recovery matters.
So does understanding your claim.

Every injury claim starts with questions: what happened, who may be responsible, what evidence is available, and how the injury has affected you. Contact Jay to discuss your situation.

Car accidents

Collisions involving passenger vehicles, including rear-end, intersection, and other roadway accidents.

Truck & commercial vehicle crashes

Claims involving trucks, delivery vehicles, and other commercial vehicles, with attention to the people, companies, and records involved.

Motorcycle, bicycle & pedestrian injuries

Accidents involving people who were riding, walking, or sharing the road with motor vehicles.

Falls & unsafe property

Injuries involving a fall or a dangerous property condition. Discuss what caused the injury, where it happened, and any available reports or photographs.

Dog bites & animal injuries

Injuries involving an animal, with review of what happened, the location, ownership information, and relevant records.

Serious & lasting injuries

Claims involving significant treatment, time away from work, ongoing limitations, or concerns about future care.

Wrongful death

A conversation about the circumstances of a loved one’s death and who may have authority to bring a claim.

Insurance & settlement questions

Review of a claim, insurer communications, or a proposed release so you can understand the questions that need to be resolved before a decision.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Before you reach out.

What if I am not sure who was at fault?

You can still request an appointment. Share what you know and any available evidence so Jay can assess responsibility and identify unanswered questions.

What if an insurer has already contacted me?

Bring the correspondence, claim number, and any proposed settlement or release. Let Jay know whether you have signed documents or received payment.

Can I discuss a claim while still receiving treatment?

Yes. Explain your current treatment, what your providers have told you, and any ongoing limitations. The legal evaluation can take account of what is known and what remains uncertain.

What compensation might be available?

Depending on the facts and applicable law, a claim may involve medical expenses, lost income, pain, limitations, or other losses. Availability and value require an individual evaluation; no amount is promised.

How do fees work?

The firm will discuss the proposed fee arrangement, case expenses, scope of work, and engagement terms for your particular matter. Ask about these before deciding whether to hire Jay.
